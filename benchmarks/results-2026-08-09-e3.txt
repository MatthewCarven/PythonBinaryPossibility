 2. WHY v1 COLLAPSED — the running model is pinned by its laggards
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  running   median eligible set     1 of 256   miss rate 98.1%   values at the minimum:   1
  windowed  median eligible set   129 of 256   miss rate  2.1%   values at the minimum:   4

  The 2%-perturbed data still has a final count spread of 2 out of 256 draws each —
  it is STILL nearly balanced. The data was never the problem. Eligibility
  anchored to min() is a max-statistic, and max-statistics are pinned by
  their single worst outlier. One value that stops being drawn holds `lo`
  down forever and the eligible set degenerates to that one laggard.

 4. SCALE — the global saving vanishes with file size. Does the local one?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
          size     local (bits)   global (bits)  local/global  local as % of file
         4,096            5,824             846            7x             17.77%
        65,536           93,185           1,354           69x             17.77%
     1,048,576        1,490,959           1,864          800x             17.77%
    16,777,216       23,855,347           2,374       10,048x             17.77%

  The global saving grows as ~(A-1)/2*log2(N) and is gone by 16 MB.
  The local saving is LINEAR in N — it is a constant 17.8% of the file,
  forever. That is the structural difference, not a bigger constant.

 5b. THE SHARPER CONTROL — does the WINDOW alone explain the gain?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WindowedBalance differs from Order0Coder in two ways at once: it models
  balance, and it models locally. WindowedOrder0 has the locality and no
  balance. Anything it matches was never about balance.

  stream                     order-0  win order-0  win balance  balance adds
  perfect order=1            0.9980x      0.9835x      1.2161x       +1.556b
  2% substituted             0.9980x      0.9837x      1.1696x       +1.292b
  5% substituted             0.9980x      0.9840x      1.1287x       +1.042b
  counter mod 256            0.9980x      0.9835x      1.2161x       +1.556b
  Mersenne Twister           0.9983x      0.9945x      1.0000x       +0.045b

  On balance-constrained data the window alone gains NOTHING (it is worse
  than the global model — resetting a frequency model on a flat histogram
  just costs you the relearning). All of the gain is balance.

  CAVEAT, and it is why the audio row of the panel is not claimed below:
  WindowedOrder0 resets to a Laplace prior of 256 pseudo-counts and then
  sees only `window` real ones, so on a 64-symbol window it is badly
  over-smoothed and is NOT a fair local-frequency baseline for peaked data.
  It is a fair baseline here, where the histogram really is flat.

  READ THIS TABLE WITH 5b's CAVEAT. The two rows with a high miss rate and
  a peaked histogram -- real audio raw and residuals -- are NOT claimed as
  balance wins. At a 33-44% miss rate the escape flag has stopped being an
  escape and become a hot/cold frequency split, which is the OPPOSITE of
  what this model claims to be doing. Resolving that needs a properly aged
  local frequency baseline, not a reset one. Open.
  The rows that ARE clean: perfect balance and counter (+1.44b, 0% miss),
  and the two negative rows -- text and shuffled enumeration -- where
  balance honestly LOSES to a plain frequency model.

 7. THE RACK — what an independent coin per step does to a 16-step bar
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PsynthRack.collapse() flips one coin per undecided step.

  independent coin     mean  8.02  sd 1.95  bars with <=4 hits  3.18%  >=12 hits  3.70%
  balanced selector    mean  8.00  sd 0.00  bars with <=4 hits  0.00%  >=12 hits  0.00%

  A balanced 16-step bar costs log2(16!)/16 = 2.7656 bits/step
  against 1.0000 for a coin — per-bit balance is the most expensive
  setting on the whole dial. For a drum pattern that expense IS the point.
