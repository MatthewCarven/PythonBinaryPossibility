 PHASE 1 BENCHMARK — does the possibility register earn its place?
==============================================================================

 Three ways to describe the same residual:
   predict+rice     no probabilities, geometric assumption   (FLAC)
   predict+register one probability per bit POSITION         (OURS)
   predict+bittree  one probability per tree NODE            (LZMA)

 Baselines are ACTUAL bytes, containers included.
 Models are analytic code lengths and pay no container cost,
 so they are flattered by a few hundred bytes of framing.

  The register is a BOUNDED-MEMORY approximation of the bit-tree:
  same binary tree, but odds per depth instead of per path. It
  gives up accuracy where the value genuinely depends on the route
  taken (analog signals) and gives up almost nothing where the
  structure is positional (counters, enumeration, telemetry) --
  while staying buildable at widths where a bit-tree cannot exist.

 KILL CRITERIA — agreed in PLAN-compression.md before running
==============================================================================

  Component question: the register (one probability per bit
  POSITION) vs the bit-tree (one per tree NODE, LZMA's model).
  Register matches or beats bit-tree on 0/9 items.

  [ not met ] loses to FLAC everywhere by a wide margin
              beats or matches FLAC on 1/4 full-depth audio items
  [ not met ] only ever wins on synthetic or sparse signals
              beats every general-purpose baseline on 1/4 full-depth real signals
  [ not met ] never beats plain Rice-coded residuals
              beats Rice on 2/6 signal+record items
  [ not met ] 'compresses' shuffled enumeration past ~1.05x
              best any coder managed on the control: 1.000x

  Explicitly NOT kill criteria:
    - losing on text -- expected, in the corpus to stay visible
    - slight expansion of incompressible input -- every codec does this

  VERDICT: no kill criteria triggered. Phase 2 is earned,
  and the tables above say which data class to aim it at.
